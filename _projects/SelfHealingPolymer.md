## Project Overview

<img src="{{ '/assets/images/5070/coverpic.png' | relative_url }}"
class="overview-image">

As part of **NBA 5070: Entrepreneurship for Engineers**, I worked on a six-person interdisciplinary team to evaluate the commercial viability of a NASA-developed self-healing polymer technology capable of autonomously repairing microscopic material damage. Rather than developing a consumer footwear product, our objective was to determine whether the technology could become a scalable platform material for performance footwear manufacturers through a business-to-business licensing model.

Throughout the semester, our team conducted **51 customer discovery interviews** with runners, footwear engineers, materials scientists, manufacturing engineers, and industry professionals to understand how material degradation affects product performance and purchasing decisions. Customer feedback ultimately led us to narrow our focus from several industrial applications to athletic footwear, where premature midsole degradation creates a clear opportunity for longer-lasting cushioning technologies.

My primary contributions focused on developing the venture's financial strategy and commercialization roadmap. I built financial projections, analyzed manufacturing cost reductions with production scale, developed the fundraising strategy, and contributed to customer discovery interviews and business model refinement throughout the project.

## Project Highlights

- Conducted customer discovery interviews alongside teammates, contributing to a total of **51 interviews** that guided product-market fit and commercialization strategy.
- Helped evaluate multiple target industries—including aerospace, advanced materials, protective equipment, and athletic footwear—before customer insights identified footwear OEMs as the strongest initial market.
- Developed five-year financial projections supporting commercialization through a B2B licensing model.
- Built manufacturing cost reduction models to evaluate how economies of scale improve profitability as production volume increases.
- Created customer acquisition cost (CAC), lifetime value (LTV), and licensing revenue models to assess long-term business viability.
- Designed the venture's fundraising roadmap, including capital requirements, funding sources, milestone planning, and deployment of investment capital toward product validation and OEM partnerships. 
- Helped refine the business model from broad material sales toward a focused B2B licensing strategy centered on strategic partnerships with athletic footwear manufacturers. 
- Presented commercialization strategy and financial analysis during the team's final investor pitch.

---

## Key Results

The final commercialization strategy proposed:

- Completion of **51 customer discovery interviews** to validate market demand and refine customer segments.
- Transition from broad industrial markets to a focused strategy targeting athletic footwear OEMs.
- A business model centered on **B2B licensing** rather than direct footwear manufacturing.
- Estimated customer lifetime values of **$500K–$5M per OEM** through multi-year licensing agreements.
- A fundraising strategy targeting **$750K–$1M** in pre-seed financing to support R&D, prototype validation, and pilot deployments with footwear manufacturers.
- A phased commercialization roadmap progressing from technical validation through OEM partnerships and long-term licensing.
