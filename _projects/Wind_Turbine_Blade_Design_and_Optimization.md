## Project Overview

<img src="{{ page.image | relative_url }}"
     alt="{{ page.title }}"
     class="overview-image">

This project focused on the aerodynamic design and optimization of a three-bladed horizontal-axis wind turbine operating under low Reynolds number conditions. Beginning with a standard NACA 2414 blade, I developed a Blade Element Momentum (BEM) model in MATLAB to optimize the blade's chord and twist distributions for maximum aerodynamic efficiency. I then validated the analytical predictions using Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) simulations in ANSYS Fluent and compared the results with experimental wind tunnel data. The final design achieved an improved predicted power coefficient while maintaining a practical, manufacturable blade geometry.

---

## Engineering Highlights
- Designed and optimized a horizontal-axis wind turbine blade using Blade Element Momentum (BEM) theory.
- Developed MATLAB scripts to generate and evaluate optimized chord and twist distributions across the blade span.
- Built CFD simulations in ANSYS Fluent using a rotating reference frame and SST *k-ω* turbulence model.
- Validated simulation results against experimental wind tunnel data to evaluate model accuracy.
- Compared the aerodynamic performance of the baseline and optimized blade designs using power coefficient (Cp) and tip-speed ratio analyses.
- Produced a complete digital design package consisting of analytical models, CFD simulations, optimization code, and engineering documentation.
