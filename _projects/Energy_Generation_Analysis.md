## Project Overview

<img src="{{ '/assets/images/5010/isone_coverpic.png' | relative_url }}"
class="overview-image">

As part of **MAE 5010: Future Energy Systems**, I worked with a team of five peers to develop a quantitative systems-level strategy for transitioning the ISO New England electric grid to a fully electrified, low-carbon power system by 2050. The project combined publicly available datasets from ISO New England and the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) with engineering modeling to evaluate current grid operations, forecast future electricity demand, and design a balanced resource portfolio capable of supporting large-scale electrification. 

The analysis examined how transportation and building electrification will reshape regional electricity demand, with particular attention to seasonal load growth, renewable integration, storage requirements, transmission expansion, and long-term system reliability. MATLAB was used to model future demand growth, evaluate generation scenarios, and visualize the effects of different planning assumptions on grid performance.

---

## Engineering Highlights

- Developed quantitative projections of ISO New England electricity demand through **2050** using MATLAB-based growth models.

- Evaluated seasonal and hourly electricity demand patterns to identify future reliability constraints caused by widespread electrification.

- Modeled transitions from a summer-peaking grid toward winter peak demand driven by electric heating and electric vehicles.

- Designed a proposed low-carbon generation portfolio incorporating offshore wind, solar, nuclear, battery storage, hydropower, and Canadian hydroelectric imports.

- Performed resource adequacy analysis to determine the generation and storage capacity required to maintain grid reliability during future peak demand events.

- Investigated operational challenges including duck-curve behavior, renewable intermittency, transmission bottlenecks, and seasonal energy storage.

- Evaluated strategies for reducing peak electricity costs through demand response, battery energy shifting, and replacement of high-cost peaker plants.

- Produced a comprehensive engineering report integrating technical analysis, forecasting models, economic considerations, and long-term infrastructure planning. 

---

## Key Results

The proposed 2050 electrification strategy projected:

- Annual electricity demand increasing from **118 TWh** today to approximately **143 TWh** by 2050.

- Winter peak demand surpassing summer peak demand due to electrified heating.

- Offshore wind becoming the dominant new generation resource with approximately **15–20 GW** of installed capacity.

- Short-duration battery storage increasing to **10–15 GW** to mitigate daily renewable variability.

- Long-duration storage and expanded Canadian hydroelectric imports providing seasonal reliability during prolonged winter demand events.

- A diversified low-carbon resource portfolio capable of approaching annual energy balance while significantly reducing dependence on fossil-fuel generation under optimistic deployment assumptions.
