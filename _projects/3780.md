## Project Overview

class="overview-image">

As part of **MAE 3780: Mechatronics**, I worked on a three-person team to design, fabricate, and test an autonomous robot capable of navigating a class-specific competition arena and collecting cubes. The project combined mechanical design, embedded electronics, and software development, with the robot required to autonomously follow boundaries while competing against other robots.

I led the mechanical design of the robot, developing the chassis layout, custom CAD components, and overall system integration while working closely with teammates on electronics and controls. Throughout the project we iteratively refined the design through prototyping, testing, and competition preparation. 

---

## Mechanical Design

The robot was designed around two main objectives:
- maximize cube collection and retention
- remain durable during collisions with competing robots

The final design included an open-front acrylic chassis that guided cubes into an enclosed collection area while protective barriers prevented cubes from becoming trapped beneath the robot. I designed the custom enclosure components and integrated them with the drivetrain, electronics, and sensor package while balancing weight, rigidity, and manufacturability.

During development we also evaluated articulated collection arms using 3D-printed hinges before ultimately replacing them with a fixed rigid assembly that proved to be significantly more reliable in testing.

## Navigation & Controls

Instead of pursuing a more complicated sensing strategy, we intentionally simplified the control system by relying on two QTI sensors to detect the arena boundary.

The robot followed a repeatable path by detecting the black border, executing timed turns, and continuously sweeping the field to collect cubes. Through repeated testing we calibrated turn durations to compensate for unequal motor performance and improve navigation accuracy. We ultimately chose to eliminate the color sensor after discovering it was highly sensitive to shadows and offered little competitive advantage.

## Design Iteration & Testing

A large portion of the project involved refining the robot through iterative testing.

Key improvements included:
- calibrating turning behavior to compensate for unequal motor speeds
- simplifying the collection mechanism by replacing hinged arms with a rigid design
- adding protective barriers to improve cube retention
- optimizing chassis robustness for collisions during competition

These iterations improved repeatability while reducing failure modes that arose due to unnecessary mechanical complexity.
